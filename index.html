The problem

By the time you can see it, it's already too late

Red Palm Weevil larvae feed inside the trunk for months before any external sign appears. Farmers currently rely on manual trunk-tapping, trained sniffer dogs, or waiting for visible damage — by then, the palm is usually beyond saving.

The solution

A cheap sensor, a trained ear, an instant decision

NakhlaGuard listens to trunk vibration through a low-cost contact sensor, and a machine learning model trained to recognize the acoustic signature of larvae feeding inside the wood — long before the palm shows any outward sign. The system classifies severity across four stages and sends an instant treatment recommendation.

How it works

From vibration to decision, in seconds

01

Listen

A low-cost sound sensor is mounted on the trunk, wired to a microcontroller.

02

Analyze

An AI model reads the frequency pattern and classifies the palm: healthy, early, moderate, or advanced infestation.

03

Decide

An instant treatment recommendation, with an estimated cost-of-inaction versus cost-of-treatment.

04

Alert

A real-time notification reaches the farmer the moment a reading crosses a risk threshold.

Agentic AI
